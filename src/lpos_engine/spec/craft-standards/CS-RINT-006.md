## Purpose

Govern technology landscape, maturity, standards, ecosystem, adoption, and weak-signal research before hands-on technical evaluation.

## Capability-first framing

Define the required capability and operating context before naming vendors or technologies. Include functional and non-functional constraints, acceptable maturity, time horizon, and consuming decision.

## Landscape requirements

Map approaches, standards, protocols, commercial products, open-source projects, services, research-stage methods, substitutes, and the current solution.

## Maturity requirements

Evaluate technical maturity, production evidence, release stability, maintainership, governance, documentation, ecosystem, interoperability, operational burden, observability, failure behavior, security history, privacy, licensing, lock-in, exit, skill availability, and sustained adoption.

Do not hide divergent dimensions inside one score.

## Benchmark integrity

Record task, dataset, version, hardware, environment, configuration, comparator, metric, sponsor, reproducibility, and applicability. Reject headline comparisons from incompatible conditions.

## Required handoff

Technology Intelligence may recommend a hands-on benchmark, proof of concept, architecture spike, security review, legal review, cost model, or monitor-and-wait decision. It may not certify production fit without the qualified engineering and assurance evidence.

## Rejection conditions

Reject:

- popularity as readiness;
- demo as production evidence;
- stars or funding as quality;
- vendor benchmark marketing as neutral truth;
- new technology without material advantage over a working alternative;
- and production recommendations without hands-on evaluation.
