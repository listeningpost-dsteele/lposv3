# Product Management Standard

## Required orientation

Begin with the approved objective, decision owner, authority, current product reality, intended user or actor, and the decision the work must improve.

A feature request is not sufficient product intent.

## Evidence integrity

Separate:

- Sourced fact
- Direct observation
- Behavioral evidence
- Research finding
- Expert judgment
- Inference
- Assumption
- Preference
- Principal directive

Do not fabricate users, personas, quotes, demand, metrics, or validation.

## Product framing

Define:

- User or actor
- Job or objective
- Context or trigger
- Current behavior
- Pain, risk, friction, or opportunity
- Current workaround
- Desired behavior change
- Desired outcome
- Evidence
- Constraints
- Success evidence

## Alternatives and decision quality

Consider real alternatives, including no change and experiment first.

State the selected option, why it wins, material tradeoffs, invalidation conditions, reversibility, and next decision point.

## Product behavior

Define externally meaningful behavior before implementation.

Cover the material user and system states required for real use, including permissions, failure, retry, recovery, cancellation, and success where applicable.

## Scope and priority

Define scope and non-goals.

Prefer the smallest coherent end-to-end outcome. Do not reduce scope by leaving nonfunctional promises, dead controls, or unverifiable behavior.

Prioritize using explicit user value, strategic fit, evidence, effort, risk, dependencies, opportunity cost, and learning. A scoring framework may inform judgment but may not replace it.

## Acceptance and measurement

Write acceptance criteria in domain language.

Define success evidence that can change a decision. State baseline, population, period, source, limitations, review date, and next action.

## Boundaries

Product Management does not replace:

- Strategy
- Research
- Experience Design
- Engineering
- Data and Analytics
- Finance
- Market and Growth
- Security and Privacy
- Legal and Compliance
- Operations
- Independent Assurance
- Chip

## Release discipline

A product artifact is not complete because a document exists.

Material work requires exact artifact identity, qualified independent product review, outcome review, and traceability to downstream verification.

## Banned product-management patterns

Reject:

- Feature lists without a product problem
- Speculative personas presented as evidence
- Roadmap theater
- Requirement documents that merely restate implementation
- Vanity metrics
- Fake precision
- Happy-path-only behavior
- “Best practice” without context
- Competitor copying without product rationale
- Prioritization scores without judgment
- Scope that cannot produce a complete outcome
- Product claims unsupported by the actual product
- Completion based on configuration, mockups, or green unit tests alone
