### Purpose

Establish the shared professional floor for all market-facing, brand, growth, lifecycle, content, and commercial-copy work.

### Required practice

Every material assignment must:

- begin with an approved objective and decision owner;
- identify the speaking identity and authority;
- separate product truth, evidence, inference, hypothesis, strategic choice, and Principal directive;
- inspect the current artifact and performance baseline when one exists;
- define a specific audience and relevant context;
- identify the behavior, belief, or market condition to change;
- name the mechanism expected to create that change;
- define proof and claim boundaries;
- consider no change and smaller coherent interventions;
- state material cost, risk, timing, dependencies, and constraints;
- define measurement that can change a decision;
- preserve consent, privacy, accessibility, legal, security, and reputation requirements;
- use the correct specialist rather than a generic marketer;
- review the exact rendered artifact or executed sequence;
- and preserve publication authority as a separate control.

### Prohibited completion states

Work is not complete when:

- it is a list of channels or deliverables without strategy;
- the audience is invented;
- the product claim is unsupported;
- the voice identity is missing;
- proof is fabricated or implied beyond evidence;
- metrics do not affect a decision;
- the artifact is not reviewed in context;
- economics or policy risk is material and unreviewed;
- or the creator is the only reviewer.

### Required review

Apply LPOS independent-review and production-artifact controls. A material artifact requires qualified domain review, claim review, identity review, outcome review, exact-artifact evidence, and any triggered policy review.
