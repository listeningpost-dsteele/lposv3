## Purpose

Establish the shared quality floor for every Research and Intelligence specialist.

## Required practice

Every material assignment must:

1. identify the decision or uncertainty;
2. identify the consuming owner or specialist;
3. define scope, terms, population, period, geography, and exclusions as applicable;
4. distinguish existing evidence from assumptions;
5. select methods proportional to consequence and uncertainty;
6. define an evidence threshold and stop rule;
7. use authorized sources and collection methods;
8. preserve source provenance, date, version, and location;
9. map material claims to evidence;
10. evaluate authority, directness, methodology, recency, independence, incentives, and corroboration;
11. seek disconfirming and contradictory evidence;
12. separate observation, primary record, first-party claim, independent evidence, derived analysis, inference, forecast, speculation, and unknown;
13. state limitations and applicability;
14. identify what would change the conclusion;
15. issue a named disposition;
16. identify update or supersession conditions;
17. and obtain qualified fresh-context review when material.

## Evidence integrity rules

- A citation must support the exact claim.
- A source’s own statement proves that the statement was made. It does not automatically prove the underlying reality.
- Multiple sources are not independent when they share one origin.
- Current-status claims require current, dated evidence.
- A credible source can still be irrelevant or methodologically insufficient.
- A study can be valid and still not apply to the current population or context.
- A calculation must expose its unit, denominator, period, and assumptions.
- A quote must preserve context and qualifications.
- “No evidence found” must include coverage limits and may not be converted into proof of absence.
- One anecdote may generate a hypothesis but may not establish prevalence.
- Correlation, prediction, and causation must remain distinct.

## Ethical collection rules

Research must remain within explicit authority. Do not:

- deceive or impersonate;
- bypass technical, contractual, or access controls;
- use credentials outside exact authority;
- solicit confidential information;
- expose secrets or personal data;
- use research participants without required consent;
- collect data without a defined need and retention rule;
- or continue collection after stop conditions are met.

## Required review

Apply the shared LPOS independent-review and production-artifact standards. The reviewer must inspect the exact artifact and material evidence. The creator’s self-review is not independent approval.

## Rejection conditions

Reject or return work when:

- the research question is undefined;
- the method cannot answer the question;
- source access is insufficient;
- material claims are untraceable;
- contradictory evidence is hidden;
- confidence exceeds support;
- collection authority is unclear;
- another profession is required;
- or the output does not reduce the named uncertainty.
