# Content Design Standard

## Functional purpose

Every content element must support a user question, decision, action, status, prevention need, or recovery need.

Remove text that exists only to fill space or describe internal implementation.

## Product truth

Content must be grounded in approved product or service behavior, authoritative terminology, and verified constraints. Do not invent capability, timing, security, privacy, legal rights, or guarantees.

## Terminology

Choose terms based on user comprehension, domain accuracy, consistency, and context. Keep internal agent, guild, runtime, orchestration, queue, and implementation language out of customer experiences unless users genuinely need it.

## Actions

Action labels name the action and object. Material consequences, external effects, financial effects, or irreversibility must be clear.

## Forms

Every field has a reason, label, requirement status, input expectation, validation, error, privacy need, and recovery path. Placeholder text is not the only label.

## Errors and recovery

Explain what happened, whether data or work is safe, what the user can do, what the system will do, and how recovery works.

Do not blame the user or use generic failure language when a specific condition is known.

## Status and notifications

Status must be truthful. Notifications must have a meaningful event, audience, channel, timing, action, deduplication rule, and owner.

Do not simulate activity or create noise.

## Tone

Apply approved voice without sacrificing accuracy, clarity, safety, or trust. Sensitive and consequential moments are not brand-performance opportunities.

## Contextual review

Review content in the exact flow, state, layout, viewport, and assistive-technology order. Strings in a spreadsheet are not sufficient when context affects meaning.
