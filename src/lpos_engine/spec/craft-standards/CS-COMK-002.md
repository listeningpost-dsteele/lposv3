# Executive Communication Standard

Executive communication must let the reader understand what matters, why it matters, what is known, what is uncertain, and what action or decision follows.

Requirements:

- order by decision leverage, not chronology;
- lead with decision, material change, risk, opportunity, preparation, or action;
- remove routine status that changes nothing;
- separate facts, decisions, recommendations, forecasts, and assumptions;
- use the approved executive identity and voice;
- state ownership and timing for next actions;
- preserve dissent and uncertainty when material;
- avoid agent narration, bureaucratic filler, and generic executive tone;
- and require exact-context editorial review.

A briefing is not complete because it is concise. It is complete when it is decision-useful and faithful to the underlying evidence and authority.
