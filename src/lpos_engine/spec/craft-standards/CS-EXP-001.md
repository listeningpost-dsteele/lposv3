# Experience Design Standard

## Required orientation

Experience work begins with an approved objective, intended person or actor, real task, context, desired action, trust requirement, current artifact or service reality, known evidence, and required outcome.

A visual request does not remove the obligation to understand the task. A requested solution is not evidence that the solution is appropriate.

## Baseline and preservation

Before materially changing an existing customer-facing artifact or service:

- Inspect the current experience.
- Capture material desktop and mobile baselines when applicable.
- Record the current journey and behavior.
- Identify approved strengths.
- Identify the smallest coherent problem to correct.
- Compare the candidate against the exact approved baseline.

A redesign must not erase working behavior, brand equity, recognizable patterns, or useful content without explicit rationale and approval.

## Evidence integrity

Separate:

- Observation
- Participant statement
- Behavioral data
- Support or operational record
- Product fact
- Stakeholder request
- Expert judgment
- Inference
- Assumption
- Principal directive

Do not fabricate users, quotes, behavior, proof, implementation, or validation.

## Complete experience

Design the relevant journey, not only the central screen. Address as applicable:

- Entry
- Orientation
- Navigation
- Primary task
- Waiting and loading
- Empty and no-result states
- Partial completion
- Permissions
- Validation and system errors
- Retry and recovery
- Cancellation
- Expiration
- Success
- Receipt or audit evidence
- Post-completion state
- Cross-channel follow-up

## Responsive and input-mode behavior

Define material viewport and input-mode behavior. Preserve task priority, content order, focus order, readable hierarchy, control access, and recovery.

Do not approve horizontal overflow, clipped required content, unreachable controls, or a mobile order that prioritizes low-value content over the user’s task.

## Content and interaction

Use real or representative content. Content must participate in the design of navigation, forms, actions, status, errors, recovery, and decisions.

Placeholder text may be used for early structure exploration but cannot support final design approval when content length, meaning, proof, or hierarchy is material.

## Visual quality

Visual decisions must support comprehension, hierarchy, trust, brand fit, state recognition, and action.

Reject design patterns that are present only because they are common in generated work or current templates, including:

- Unrequested chat shells
- Command centers
- Generic dashboards
- Arbitrary bento layouts
- Excessive cards
- Excessive pills
- Neon AI styling
- Gradient fog
- Fake activity feeds
- Decorative proof
- Repeated section rhythms
- Unsupported metrics or badges
- Visual hierarchy that could be pasted onto a competitor unchanged

Name the observable defect and correct the smallest coherent set of failures. Do not claim an artifact was generated by AI based on appearance.

## Honest representation

A prototype, static mockup, staged environment, and production release must be labeled accurately.

Do not present:

- Dead controls as functional
- Simulated activity as live
- Placeholder data as customer evidence
- Fabricated testimonials, logos, reviews, or metrics
- Mock success states as completed backend behavior
- Unverified interactions as working

## Rendered review

Review the exact rendered artifact and primary journey. Source files, component names, or written explanations are not enough.

Material review requires the relevant combination of:

- Desktop evidence
- Mobile evidence
- Interaction evidence
- State evidence
- Content evidence
- Accessibility design evidence
- Baseline comparison
- Fresh-context domain review
- Independent production verification

## Completion

Experience work is complete only when the required artifact exists, the intended task is coherent, material states are covered, responsive behavior is explicit, content is real enough to evaluate, accessibility requirements are integrated, the artifact is rendered, and qualified review passes.
