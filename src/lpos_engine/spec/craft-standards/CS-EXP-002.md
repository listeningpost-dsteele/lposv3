# UX Research Standard

## Decision focus

Begin with the decision or experience uncertainty the research must improve. State what evidence would change the decision and what evidence would be insufficient.

## Existing-evidence audit

Inspect prior research, support records, product evidence, analytics, and operational records before recruiting participants. Do not run redundant research for the appearance of rigor.

## Method fit

Select the smallest credible method for the question. Distinguish exploratory, generative, evaluative, behavioral, attitudinal, qualitative, and quantitative evidence.

## Participant integrity

Define participant logic based on relevant behavior, context, experience, and accessibility needs. Do not claim representation beyond the method and sample.

Employees, stakeholders, friends, and language models are not substitutes for the intended population unless the study explicitly concerns those groups.

## Consent and privacy

Define consent, recording, data collection, storage, access, retention, deletion, withdrawal, and sensitive-data handling before collection.

## Neutral protocol

Avoid leading, loaded, compound, and solution-teaching questions. Prefer observed behavior and demonstrated tasks over hypothetical preference when possible.

## Analysis integrity

Separate observation, participant statement, interpretation, hypothesis, implication, contradiction, and anomaly. Preserve meaningful disagreement.

## Reporting strength

Every finding states its evidence, context, strength, limitations, contradictions, implication, and what would change it.

Do not fabricate quotes, count qualitative comments as population statistics, or write “users want” beyond the evidence.

## Synthetic evidence prohibition

Language models, synthetic personas, and generated transcripts may help create hypotheses or rehearse a protocol. They are never user research evidence and may not be described as participant findings.
