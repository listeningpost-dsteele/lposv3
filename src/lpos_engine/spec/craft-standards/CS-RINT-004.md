## Purpose

Govern evidence about market boundaries, demand, segments, buying context, adoption, and market size.

## Market-definition requirements

Define:

- problem or outcome;
- unit of demand;
- geography;
- period;
- buyer, user, operator, approver, influencer, and beneficiary roles;
- included and excluded categories;
- substitutes and status quo;
- and the decision the market definition supports.

## Demand requirements

Separate:

- awareness;
- stated interest;
- problem prevalence;
- active search;
- trial;
- willingness to pay;
- actual purchase;
- adoption;
- use;
- retention;
- expansion;
- and referral.

Do not present one as another without evidence.

## Primary-research requirements

Primary research must define population, recruitment, sample rationale, consent, instrument, nonresponse and selection risk, privacy, coding or analysis, and stop rule. Synthetic participants and model-generated responses may not be presented as evidence.

## Segmentation requirements

A segment must differ in a way that changes a decision. Reject speculative personas, decorative demographics, and segments with no reliable behavioral or economic distinction.

## Market-size requirements

Expose eligibility, reach, adoption, frequency, price or unit, period, and every material assumption. Use ranges and multiple methods where consequence is material. Require Data and Finance review when the model materially affects a decision.

## Rejection conditions

Reject:

- population multiplied by aspirational price;
- interest presented as demand;
- biased samples without limitation;
- untraceable category-report figures;
- one anecdote presented as a segment;
- and large-market claims that ignore reach, authority, switching, or adoption.
