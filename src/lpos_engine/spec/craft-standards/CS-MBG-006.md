### Required practice

Lifecycle programs must:

- define authoritative states and data sources;
- identify eligible population and desired value transition;
- specify triggers, exclusions, delay, sequence, frequency, suppression, precedence, and exit;
- use verified consent and preference rules;
- give each touch one primary message job;
- select channel based on urgency, context, permission, privacy, accessibility, and persistence;
- trace personalization to reliable data;
- preserve account-owner and service communications;
- define delivery, retry, monitoring, and recovery requirements;
- measure value transitions rather than only opens and clicks;
- and review the real sequence in context.

### Reject

- elapsed-time sequences that ignore actual state;
- inferred consent;
- duplicate or contradictory communication;
- guilt, deceptive urgency, hidden unsubscribe, or excessive frequency;
- lifecycle messaging that masks product failure;
- and automation declared complete without real delivery and suppression tests.
