# Business Case and Investment Analysis Standard

A business case compares real alternatives, including no action, against a credible counterfactual. It uses incremental future costs and benefits rather than sunk cost or desired narrative.

Include implementation, migration, training, review, support, maintenance, failure, compliance, opportunity, and exit costs where material. Count a labor-time benefit as cash savings only when spend, hiring, contractor use, constrained capacity, or output changes.

Show timing, cash-flow structure, payback, break-even, NPV or another appropriate method, adverse scenarios, sensitivities, reversibility, and benefit-realization evidence.

Do not monetize every non-financial factor to force a score. Preserve legal, security, privacy, customer, mission, and strategic constraints as explicit decision factors.
