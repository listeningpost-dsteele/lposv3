# Web Experience Design Standard

## Commercial inputs

A final public web design requires the intended audience, desired action, approved positioning, brand specification, voice specification, product truth, proof, objections, and content.

Design may use placeholders for early exploration but may not invent the commercial substance.

## Journey and architecture

Design entry expectations, orientation, audience questions, trust questions, exploration, primary and secondary action, forms or conversion, confirmation, and follow-up.

Organize the site around audience needs rather than the internal company structure.

## Page definition

Each material page identifies its audience intent, page promise, primary question, supporting questions, proof, action, content sequence, responsive order, and success condition.

## Distinctive visual execution

Apply approved brand through deliberate typography, layout, spacing, color, imagery, iconography, density, pacing, surface, and motion.

Reject generic SaaS heroes, cloned proof bands, arbitrary bento layouts, excessive card grids, gradient fog, neon AI styling, and decorative dashboards that do not serve the content or task.

## Proof integrity

Unsupported metrics, badges, ratings, reviews, testimonials, customer logos, guarantees, urgency, and activity are blockers.

## Responsive and accessible web

Define navigation, reading order, forms, tables, comparisons, code, media, long content, calls to action, focus, contrast, zoom, motion, and nonvisual meaning across material viewports.

## Performance-aware design

Identify design choices that materially affect load, rendering, media weight, motion, and interaction responsiveness. Engineering owns implementation and measurement; Design may not ignore foreseeable performance cost.

## Live-journey review

Final review must inspect the rendered material routes, forms, navigation, interactions, mobile behavior, content, and proof. A homepage screenshot is not a website review.
