# Service Design Standard

## Service boundary

Define where the service begins and ends, the intended outcome, actors, channels, and excluded scope.

## Current-state evidence

Inspect actual service behavior rather than relying only on policy or process documentation.

## Blueprint completeness

Map user actions, touchpoints, frontstage work, backstage work, support processes, systems and providers, information, waits, handoffs, ownership, failure, recovery, and measures.

## Root-cause discipline

Distinguish interface failures, service failures, policy conflicts, ownership gaps, information loss, operational constraints, and integration failures.

Do not propose a screen change when the root problem is service delivery.

## Target-state honesty

A target-state blueprint is experience intent, not operating evidence. Do not claim readiness until the required product, operational, policy, automation, data, and assurance work exists.

## Validation

Use scenario walkthroughs, role-play, service prototypes, pilots, tabletop exercises, research, or failure exercises appropriate to the risk and uncertainty.

## Handoffs

Every material target-state change must identify the correct professional owner, authority, dependency, evidence, and completion condition.
