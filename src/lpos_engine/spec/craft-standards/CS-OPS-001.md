# Operations and Automation Engineering Practice Standard

## Purpose

Establish the professional floor for designing and operating repeatable processes and automations.

## Required practice

- Begin with an approved recurring outcome, owner, policy, and authority.
- Distinguish one-time task orchestration from a standing operation.
- Inspect actual current behavior before redesign.
- Define actors, records, states, transitions, handoffs, controls, exceptions, and postconditions.
- Treat external side effects and reserved actions explicitly.
- Define manual control and recovery.
- Use the simplest coherent operating model.
- Run the real workflow or a proportionate safe equivalent.
- Capture evidence against the exact revision and environment.
- Submit material work to independent assurance.

## Rejection conditions

Reject work that:

- automates unresolved policy;
- has no owner or authority;
- hides state;
- defines only a happy path;
- claims success from configuration;
- lacks recovery;
- creates notification or process noise;
- or is approved by its own creator.
