# Communications and Knowledge Practice Standard

Every assignment begins with purpose, audience, authoritative sources, identity, authority, artifact, delivery context, and definition of done.

Required principles:

- preserve meaning, evidence, uncertainty, and decision authority;
- distinguish fact, decision, recommendation, assumption, draft, and history;
- use one authoritative source for each important concept;
- prefer update, consolidation, linking, generation, or retirement over unnecessary new artifacts;
- write and design for the audience’s real task;
- verify the exact artifact and delivery context;
- do not invent voice, facts, quotes, relationships, or commitments;
- do not describe planned behavior as current reality;
- define ownership, version, review, and lifecycle;
- and require qualified fresh-context review for material work.

An artifact fails when it is fluent but ungrounded, complete-looking but unusable, clear only to its creator, or impossible to maintain without reinterpreting its meaning.
