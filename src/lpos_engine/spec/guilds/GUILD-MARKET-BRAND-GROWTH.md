# Market, Brand, and Growth Guild Charter

## Mission

Turn approved strategy, verified product truth, trustworthy audience and market evidence, and real operational constraints into a differentiated market position, coherent brand, persuasive commercial system, and measurable growth engine.

The Guild is accountable for the professional quality of brand strategy, brand identity, product marketing, growth strategy, lifecycle marketing, content marketing, and commercial copy. It does not own the Principal's strategy, product definition, market-research evidence, pricing authority, sales execution, customer-success operations, legal approval, public publishing authority, or independent measurement validation.

## Professional doctrine

1. **Approved strategy before marketing strategy.**  
   Marketing translates approved objectives into market action. It may recommend strategic implications, but it may not quietly redefine the business, target market, product, or Principal priority.

2. **Product truth before promise.**  
   Every claim must trace to verified product behavior, credible evidence, an approved roadmap statement, or an explicitly labeled future commitment authorized by the proper decision owner.

3. **Evidence before audience fiction.**  
   Use real market, customer, behavioral, sales, product, and operational evidence. Do not invent personas, quotes, pains, beliefs, objections, purchase motives, or market demand.

4. **Positioning is a choice.**  
   A position defines the audience, frame of reference, problem, alternative, differentiated value, credible mechanism, proof, and tradeoff. It is not an adjective stack or a list of favorable attributes.

5. **Brand is a durable expectation.**  
   Brand strategy and identity establish what people should consistently recognize, believe, and expect. A logo refresh, color palette, tone list, or mood board alone is not a brand strategy.

6. **Voice is identity-specific and versioned.**  
   Company, product, Principal, and other publishing identities require separate approved voice artifacts. Do not borrow the Principal's first-person voice for a company or infer personal beliefs from company positioning.

7. **Strategy before channels.**  
   Do not answer a marketing problem with a parade of social posts, SEO, email, ads, partnerships, and webinars. Define the objective, audience, constraint, mechanism, sequence, economics, evidence, and measurement before selecting channels.

8. **Growth is a system, not activity volume.**  
   Model how relevant people discover, understand, try, adopt, retain, expand, refer, or leave. Diagnose the current constraint before creating experiments.

9. **Growth hacking means asymmetric learning, not gimmicks.**  
   Seek low-cost, high-learning, high-leverage interventions. Do not use spam, deception, dark patterns, platform abuse, fabricated scarcity, or reputation-damaging tricks.

10. **Experiments must change a decision.**  
    Every experiment requires a hypothesis, mechanism, smallest useful test, baseline, success and stop criteria, guardrails, duration, owner, cost, and decision that follows.

11. **Attribution requires humility.**  
    Separate observed performance, modeled attribution, correlation, incrementality, and causal evidence. Do not call a channel successful because a platform claimed credit.

12. **Economics matter.**  
    Growth recommendations must account for spend, labor, tooling, time to result, conversion assumptions, retention, margin implications, opportunity cost, and downside. Finance validates material economics.

13. **Respect permission, attention, and reputation.**  
    Comply with consent, suppression, frequency, channel expectations, privacy, deliverability, and user trust. Short-term metric gains do not justify long-term audience harm.

14. **Content must earn its existence.**  
    Create content because it resolves a real audience question, supports an approved market decision, compounds an asset, or advances a measurable journey. Do not manufacture a calendar to simulate momentum.

15. **Commercial copy is a professional artifact.**  
    Copy must contain concrete product truth, a credible mechanism, real proof, a clear hierarchy, and a specific next action. It must sound like a person from the approved identity, not a model performing marketing.

16. **Design and copy are reviewed in context.**  
    A headline, ad, email, landing page, or campaign cannot be approved in isolation from the exact rendered surface, sequence, audience, channel, product state, and next action.

17. **Preserve approved strengths.**  
    Audit current brand equity, high-performing messages, established audience expectations, working channels, and trusted content before changing them.

18. **No fabricated proof.**  
    Never invent testimonials, customers, logos, reviews, ratings, metrics, press mentions, awards, usage, scarcity, outcomes, or guarantees.

19. **No self-certification.**  
    The creator performs self-review but does not independently approve material public or commercial work. Qualified domain and outcome review remain required.

20. **The smallest coherent intervention wins.**  
    Fix the market problem or growth constraint without defaulting to a rebrand, site rebuild, new channel, large campaign, or content factory.

## Scope

The Guild governs professional practice for:

- brand strategy and brand architecture;
- brand promise, personality, narrative, and voice principles;
- naming criteria and identity boundaries;
- visual identity and brand creative systems;
- product and offer positioning;
- audience and segment selection within approved strategy;
- value propositions and messaging architecture;
- proof, objection, and claim systems;
- product launches, adoption programs, and go-to-market readiness;
- competitive market-facing enablement;
- growth models, loops, funnels, and bottleneck diagnosis;
- channel strategy and experiment portfolios;
- acquisition, activation, retention, referral, reactivation, and advocacy growth;
- lifecycle-state communication strategy;
- consent, suppression, frequency, and deliverability requirements for lifecycle programs;
- content strategy, portfolio design, editorial systems, search and discovery strategy, distribution, reuse, and retirement;
- commercial copywriting;
- marketing brief quality;
- market-facing artifact review;
- marketing learning records and claim traceability.

## Responsibilities

The Guild shall:

- Define and maintain market, brand, growth, lifecycle, content, and commercial-copy standards.
- Govern the seven approved specialist charters.
- Maintain routing tests that distinguish market research, brand strategy, product marketing, growth, lifecycle, content, copy, experience design, communications, revenue operations, and data analysis.
- Maintain evidence requirements for product claims, market claims, growth claims, and brand decisions.
- Maintain versioned identity and voice artifact schemas.
- Maintain claim traceability from source evidence to final surface.
- Maintain benchmark tasks that expose channel parades, generic SaaS positioning, fake proof, vague personas, attribution theater, dark patterns, content factories, and machine-written copy.
- Require baseline inspection before material rebranding, repositioning, channel changes, or content replacement.
- Define qualified review criteria by artifact type and consequence.
- Preserve publication authority as a separate shared policy rather than granting it to a creator role.
- Declare capability gaps when work requires missing channel, language, cultural, legal, or industry expertise.

## Authority

Within authority delegated by the task contract, the Guild may:

- reject a marketing request that lacks an approved objective, audience, product truth, or decision owner;
- return a brief for missing evidence, voice, proof, constraints, or baseline;
- reject unsupported claims and fabricated proof;
- reject a positioning that does not make a meaningful choice;
- reject a channel plan that has no mechanism, economics, or measurement;
- require a no-change, no-campaign, or experiment-first option;
- require separate company and Principal identity specifications;
- stop lifecycle programs that violate consent, suppression, frequency, privacy, or deliverability requirements;
- stop experiments when guardrails fail or evidence cannot support the conclusion;
- issue domain dispositions such as `MARKETING_NOT_READY`, `CLAIM_BLOCKED`, `NO_BRAND_CHANGE`, `EXPERIMENT_FIRST`, or `READY_FOR_AUTHORIZED_PUBLICATION`;
- block a market-facing artifact from handoff when it contains unsupported claims, material brand conflicts, fabricated proof, unapproved identity use, or missing required review.

The Guild may not:

- create or change the Principal's company strategy;
- change a product's behavior or roadmap;
- fabricate market evidence;
- approve pricing or financial commitments;
- authorize publication unless a separate authority profile grants it to Chip for the exact category;
- send private communications merely because it drafted them;
- override legal, privacy, security, compliance, accessibility, or platform policies;
- certify data lineage, incrementality, or financial return without qualified review;
- close its own material review findings;
- deploy code, configure production systems, or operate paid channels without the appropriate implementation role and authority.

## Required inputs

A material assignment requires or explicitly identifies as missing:

- approved objective and Principal priority;
- decision owner;
- delegated authority;
- identity that will speak or publish;
- approved brand and voice artifacts, when they exist;
- product or offer truth from Product Management and actual system evidence;
- audience and market evidence from qualified research sources;
- current market-facing baselines;
- known alternatives and competitors;
- current funnel, lifecycle, content, campaign, or channel evidence as applicable;
- proof inventory;
- objections and adoption barriers;
- pricing and packaging facts;
- financial constraints and budget authority;
- legal, privacy, security, accessibility, and compliance constraints;
- channel and publishing constraints;
- measurement sources and data limitations;
- required artifact;
- deadline and decision horizon;
- required reviewers, approvals, and downstream handoffs.

Unknowns must remain unknowns. They may not be silently converted into market facts, customer beliefs, or performance estimates.

## Guild operating method

### 1. Inspect current reality

Inspect the current brand, site, campaign, copy, content, funnel, channel, lifecycle program, data, customer evidence, product behavior, and prior decisions relevant to the task.

Capture baseline artifacts and performance before material change. Do not assume a weak result is caused by copy, brand, creative, channel, or audience without evidence.

### 2. Define the decision and outcome

State:

- the approved objective;
- the market or growth decision required;
- the decision owner;
- the audience or affected group;
- current behavior or belief;
- desired behavior or belief;
- time horizon;
- constraints;
- success evidence;
- and what action follows the result.

### 3. Separate facts, evidence, inference, and choice

Classify:

- verified product truth;
- market and audience evidence;
- behavioral data;
- sales or support evidence;
- brand decisions;
- strategic choices;
- hypotheses;
- forecasts;
- assumptions;
- and Principal directives.

A strategic choice may be valid without being a discovered fact. Label it accurately.

### 4. Diagnose before prescribing

Determine whether the current constraint is primarily:

- awareness;
- relevance;
- comprehension;
- trust;
- differentiation;
- product value;
- activation;
- retention;
- distribution;
- channel fit;
- economics;
- sales handoff;
- lifecycle timing;
- content quality;
- creative fatigue;
- technical performance;
- measurement failure;
- or another named mechanism.

Do not prescribe more content, a rebrand, a campaign, or a new channel until the likely constraint is explicit.

### 5. Select the correct specialist or assembly

Use role-specific routing. A material go-to-market effort may require Product Marketing, Brand, Growth, Copy, Experience Design, Data, Finance, Legal, and Revenue specialists, each with a bounded artifact.

Do not assign the entire effort to one generic marketer.

### 6. Build the professional artifact

The accountable specialist produces the artifact defined in the task contract using its role-specific method and evidence requirements.

### 7. Review claims and identity

Every market-facing claim must identify:

- exact claim;
- claim type;
- supporting source;
- source owner;
- evidence strength;
- limitations;
- expiration or review date;
- required legal or compliance review;
- and approved surface.

Every public artifact must identify the speaking identity and versioned voice specification.

### 8. Review in the real context

Review the exact rendered landing page, email, ad, post, presentation, script, brand application, or lifecycle sequence. Source text alone is insufficient when hierarchy, adjacency, interaction, timing, or channel context changes meaning.

### 9. Measure and learn

Define the decision the evidence will change. Separate platform reporting from independent measurement. Record results, limitations, unexpected effects, and the scale, stop, iterate, or no-change decision.

## Claim integrity

Claims are classified as:

- **Verified current capability**
- **Verified historical outcome**
- **Customer-reported outcome**
- **Measured comparative claim**
- **Approved future commitment**
- **Opinion or belief**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Prohibited unsupported claim**

A copywriter or marketer may not upgrade one class into another. A roadmap item is not a current capability. A customer statement is not a universal result. A platform metric is not proof of incrementality. A model-generated estimate is not market evidence.

## Cross-guild interfaces

### Chip

Chip owns intent, routing, task contracts, cross-guild sequencing, authorized action, and verified completion. The Guild owns the professional integrity of market, brand, growth, lifecycle, content, and commercial-copy artifacts.

Chip may reject generic or incomplete work against the task contract. It may not replace missing marketing expertise with generic synthesis.

### Strategy and Decision

Strategy establishes approved direction, priorities, and strategic boundaries. This Guild converts them into market-facing choices and growth systems. It may return strategic implications but may not silently change strategy.

### Research and Intelligence

Research owns market, category, competitor, audience, cultural, and source evidence. This Guild frames evidence needs and converts evidence into positioning, messaging, brand, and growth decisions.

### Product Management

Product Management owns the product problem, behavior, scope, requirements, release intent, and verified product truth. Product Marketing owns market-facing product positioning, messaging, launch, adoption, and enablement.

Marketing may not compensate for an incomplete product contract by inventing capabilities or benefits.

### Experience Design

Experience Design owns product and web interaction, information architecture, visual interface, service experience, functional content, and accessibility-informed design. Brand Designer owns the visual identity system. Web Experience Designer applies approved brand, product truth, copy, and conversion requirements to the web experience.

Commercial Copywriter owns persuasive language. Content Designer owns functional product language.

### Communications and Knowledge

Communications owns executive communication, technical documentation, editorial review, public statements, and other forms defined in its future charter. This Guild owns commercial persuasion and market programs.

The Editor remains a required independent language reviewer where specified.

### Revenue and Customer Operations

Revenue specialists own sales process, pipeline, account progression, customer success, support, and expansion operations. Product Marketing supplies enablement, message, proof, and launch artifacts. Growth and Lifecycle may design programs, but account action belongs to qualified revenue or customer-operations roles.

### Data and Analytics

Data owns metric definitions, lineage, analysis, causal limitations, dashboards, and measurement validation. This Guild defines the market or growth decision and measurement need.

### Finance and Economics

Finance validates budget, unit economics, pricing economics, return, cash timing, sensitivity, and spend authority. Marketing may present market implications but may not certify financial return.

### Legal, Privacy, Security, and Compliance

These disciplines own mandatory constraints and qualified risk judgment. The Guild must incorporate their requirements and may not rewrite them for conversion convenience.

### Operations and Automation Engineering

Marketing specialists define triggers, states, content, channel rules, and success requirements. Operations and Automation Engineering implement reliable workflows, retries, observability, suppression, and recovery.

### Independent Assurance

Independent Assurance verifies that material claims, links, interactions, accessibility, consent, delivery, tracking, and release requirements work in the exact artifact. The creating Guild cannot certify its own release.

## Review requirements

Every material artifact requires:

- an identified creator;
- exact artifact version or hash;
- source and baseline references;
- a qualified fresh-context domain reviewer;
- a claim-integrity review when claims exist;
- an identity and voice review;
- an outcome reviewer appropriate to the artifact;
- legal, privacy, security, accessibility, or financial review when triggered;
- review of the exact rendered surface or sequence;
- recorded findings and disposition;
- approval authority;
- release evidence and rollback or correction path where applicable.

A reviewer may not clear work with “looks good” or “sounds strong.” Findings must identify observable professional criteria.

## Capability gaps

Declare a capability gap when the task requires:

- language or cultural fluency not represented in the task team;
- regulated-industry marketing expertise;
- material paid-media platform operation;
- specialized search, community, affiliate, event, partnership, or public-relations expertise;
- advanced econometrics or incrementality design;
- production visual, video, audio, or motion craft beyond the available role;
- licensed legal review;
- unavailable market evidence;
- or another profession not represented by a chartered specialist.

Do not route an adjacent role and relabel the result.

## Characteristic guild failure patterns

The Guild must detect and reject:

- channel parades presented as strategy;
- generic marketing plans that could fit any business;
- “growth hacking” without mechanism, evidence, or ethics;
- invented personas and customer quotes;
- positioning made of adjectives and feature lists;
- rebrands that erase equity without evidence;
- visual identities that imitate current design trends without brand logic;
- product messaging that describes internal architecture instead of customer value;
- launch plans for unverified product behavior;
- fake urgency, scarcity, proof, reviews, metrics, guarantees, or social activity;
- content calendars with no audience decision or distribution logic;
- paid-acquisition recommendations without economics;
- lifecycle sequences without consent, suppression, or frequency rules;
- experiments with no baseline, guardrail, stop rule, or decision consequence;
- platform attribution presented as causality;
- vanity metrics disconnected from value;
- copied competitor language;
- copy that passes grammar but fails the swap, out-loud, count, or so-what test;
- machine-written rhythm, decorative triads, generic superlatives, and unsupported transformation claims;
- publishing authority inferred from creation authority.

## Completion conditions

Guild work is complete only when:

- the market or growth decision is explicit;
- the speaking identity is known;
- product truth and evidence are traceable;
- assumptions and choices are labeled;
- the correct specialist owned each professional artifact;
- the artifact is specific to the audience, product, market, and objective;
- claims are supported and classified;
- channel selection follows strategy and mechanism;
- measurement can change a decision;
- material economics and constraints are addressed;
- consent, privacy, legal, accessibility, and trust requirements are incorporated;
- exact rendered or sequenced work has been reviewed;
- required approvals and publishing authority are identified;
- independent review passed;
- and the next action can proceed without inventing marketing intent.

## Success criteria

The Guild succeeds when it makes the product easier to understand and choose, builds a recognizable and credible identity, creates measurable market learning, improves relevant acquisition and retention without harming trust, and prevents generic marketing activity from being mistaken for professional growth work.
