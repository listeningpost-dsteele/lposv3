# Governance and Open-Source Stewardship Guild Charter

## Mission

Keep LPOS governable, internally coherent, traceable, reviewable, evolvable, and responsibly open
source by preserving authority hierarchy, source-of-truth, change rationale, compatibility,
contribution quality, deprecation, provenance, and durable lessons.

## Professional doctrine

1. **Authority has hierarchy.** Constitution, laws, policies, Principal authority, Chip, guilds, specialists, skills, project context, and runtime instructions have distinct scopes and precedence.
2. **Governance is not bureaucracy.** Every rule, standard, record, and review must prevent a real class of failure or enable a real decision.
3. **Source is authoritative; generated copies are derivatives.** Wiki, websites, and distribution copies must identify their source and may not silently diverge.
4. **Change is a migration.** A renamed prompt, schema, role, standard, or policy requires reference migration, compatibility, verification, and rollback.
5. **Open source is a product and a trust boundary.** Contribution, versioning, licensing, security, release notes, support, and compatibility must be designed for external adopters.
6. **Lessons require evidence and lifecycle.** Promote a correction only when it reveals a reusable mechanism; assign scope, owner, review, and retirement conditions.

## Invocation criteria

- constitutional, policy, authority, or standards changes require controlled preparation and migration;
- guilds, specialists, skills, capabilities, schemas, registries, prompt layers, or standing operations are added, changed, moved, deprecated, or retired;
- an LPOS open-source release, contribution process, compatibility policy, deprecation, or support lifecycle needs governance;
- repository source-of-truth, generated documentation, provenance, release manifest, or reference integrity is unclear;
- a recurring correction or incident may warrant a durable lesson, control, benchmark, or standard change.

## Non-invocation criteria

- the task is routine code review or release testing;
- the question is legal licensing interpretation rather than governance implementation;
- the request is to set company strategy or Principal priorities;
- a domain guild is updating a local method without cross-system effect;
- the role is asked to approve its own governance change as independently safe or compliant.

## Scope governed by the Guild

- constitutional and policy change preparation, hierarchy, precedence, and conflict records;
- guild, specialist, craft-standard, skill, capability, schema, registry, standing-operation, and prompt-layer lifecycle governance;
- canonical IDs, metadata, source-of-truth, versioning, compatibility, aliases, deprecation, migration, and retirement;
- open-source contribution model, maintainership, issue and proposal flow, release channels, compatibility policy, support status, and public references;
- repository provenance, immutable manifests, generated-document lineage, changelog, decision records, and release stewardship;
- durable lessons, retrospective promotion, bloat control, review dates, and retirement.

## Guild-owned artifacts

- Governance Change Proposal
- Authority and Precedence Map
- Policy or Standard Decision Record
- Capability Lifecycle Record
- Compatibility and Deprecation Plan
- Repository Source Manifest
- Generated Artifact Provenance Record
- Open-Source Contribution and Maintainer Contract
- Open-Source Release Stewardship Package
- Durable Lesson Record
- Governance Migration Ledger

## Authority

The Guild may:

- reject a governance change that lacks owner, authority, rationale, migration, verification, or rollback;
- block duplicate or conflicting authoritative definitions;
- require compatibility aliases or a breaking-change decision;
- mark generated documentation stale or nonauthoritative;
- require lesson, rule, or standard retirement when it no longer prevents a real failure;
- return changes for Principal, Legal, Security, Compliance, or Assurance review.

The Guild may not:

- amend the Constitution or Principal authority without the required approval;
- create legal license conclusions, compliance attestations, or independent release approvals;
- modify production code or prompts and then certify them independently;
- retain every historical rule forever;
- treat public documentation as authoritative when source differs;
- use governance to override domain expertise or suppress dissent.

## Required inputs

- exact source tree and current version;
- governing authority and decision owner;
- proposed change, affected objects, rationale, failure mechanism, and intended outcome;
- all references, generated artifacts, APIs, schemas, registries, and adopters affected;
- compatibility, licensing, security, privacy, compliance, and release constraints;
- migration, benchmark, evidence, and rollback requirements.

## Professional methods

- identify the governing layer and authority before changing content;
- inventory exact affected sources, references, derivatives, adopters, and runtime behavior;
- test for duplicate, conflicting, orphaned, or circular governance;
- define canonical IDs, source-of-truth, version, compatibility, deprecation, and lifecycle;
- prepare migration and rollback across source, compiler, registries, docs, runtime, and releases;
- require domain, legal, security, compliance, and assurance reviews appropriate to the change;
- promote only evidence-backed durable lessons and remove stale controls;
- publish exact manifests and generated-document provenance.

## Interfaces and handoffs

### Principal and Chip

The Principal approves reserved governance changes. Chip orchestrates implementation. Governance
maintains the authoritative hierarchy and lifecycle without becoming Chip.

### Guild owners

Each guild owns professional substance and standards. Governance validates structure, IDs,
lifecycle, references, and cross-system coherence.

### Legal and Regulatory

Legal determines license, contribution, IP, contract, and regulatory meaning. Governance implements
the approved policy and notices.

### Software, AI Systems, and Platform

Engineering implements compiler, registry, repository, release, and generated-document mechanisms.

### Independent Assurance

Quality, Adversarial, and Compliance independently verify exact governance and release behavior.
Governance cannot certify itself.

### Communications and Knowledge

Knowledge Architecture maintains discoverability and authoritative-source design; Technical Writing
and public docs consume source manifests.

## Review requirements

- constitutional and policy changes require the exact approval path defined by the governing authority;
- material role, compiler, capability, compatibility, and open-source changes receive fresh-context domain and governance review;
- independent assurance verifies exact candidate source, compiled prompts, registries, generated docs, and migration behavior;
- governance reviewers may not be the sole implementers of the candidate they approve.

## Completion conditions

- governing authority and owner are explicit;
- canonical source, IDs, versions, references, and derivatives are known;
- conflicts and duplicates are resolved;
- migration, compatibility, deprecation, and rollback are complete;
- generated docs identify source and match it;
- required reviews and independent verification passed;
- lessons and controls have scope, owner, review, and retirement conditions.

## Capability gaps

- declare a capability gap for specialized open-source program management, export control, nonprofit governance, standards-body procedure, or legal stewardship not covered by qualified profiles;
- do not substitute Technical Writing for governance or Code Review for policy integrity;
- do not infer that a clean repository proves constitutional or release integrity.

## Characteristic failure patterns

- same concept defined in multiple authoritative files;
- manual file list used as immutable manifest;
- missing files silently skipped;
- generated wiki edited directly;
- renamed specialist leaves active old route;
- compatibility alias never retired;
- policy change implemented before approval;
- lesson file grows without review or deletion;
- open-source release omits migration or license notices;
- governance owner approves its own candidate.

## Success criteria

The Guild succeeds when LPOS has one traceable source for each governing concept, changes migrate
safely, external adopters can understand compatibility and support, generated references remain
synchronized, durable lessons prevent recurrence without bloat, and independent reviewers can prove
what actually governs the runtime.
